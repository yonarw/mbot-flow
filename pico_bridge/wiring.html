mBot Pico Bridge

A Pico WH replaces the broken Bluetooth module: it sits in the module's place, joins your WiFi and passes the robot's serial line to the block GUI. Four wires and three resistors.

Wiring

The robot's TX line carries 5 V, which can damage the Pico. One R on top and two R below divide it to ⅔, about 3.3 V. The other three wires need no resistors.

Finding the pins

Pico: hold it with the USB plug pointing up and the chip side facing you. Every pin you need is in the top three rows. The back of the board has each pin's name printed on it, so you can double-check there.

mBot socket: the pin numbers come from Makeblock's schematic. Check them with the multimeter before wiring: with the robot on and the socket empty, pin 1 shows 5 V against pin 2. Pins 3 and 4 follow in the same row.

Order of work

  1. Flash the mBot's firmware first, over USB with nothing in the socket: ./flash.sh in the project root. The robot currently runs Makeblock's original firmware.
  2. Switch the robot off and unplug the Pico from USB.
  3. Wire everything as above. Check the divider once more: one R between P5 pin 4 and GP1, two R from GP1 to GND.
  4. Switch the robot on and watch the Pico's LED.
  5. Start the GUI (./start.sh), open “Nicht verbunden”, then Per WLAN (Pico) → Suchen → Verbinden.

Rules

  • Never connect P5 pin 4 (mBot TX) straight to the Pico.
  • Keep the Pico's USB unplugged while it is wired to the robot. Otherwise the PC's USB power flows into the robot through the Pico.
  • Before flashing the mBot over USB, pull the orange wire (P5 pin 3). Otherwise the Pico garbles the upload, just like the Bluetooth module did.
  • Your router assigned the Pico 192.168.1.50. The address can change; “Suchen” always finds the current one.
